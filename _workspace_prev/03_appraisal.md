**Frame (per user):** CV prevention in non-diabetic obesity (broadened) · **semaglutide only** · focus on
evidence + contradiction gaps.

## GRADE (per outcome)
- **MACE (3-point) reduction:** **Moderate.** One large low-RoB RCT (SELECT) + concordant meta-analyses
  (RR 0.80). Downgraded once for **indirectness**: SELECT is established-CVD (secondary prevention); true
  primary-prevention non-diabetic MACE data are extrapolated.
- **All-cause mortality reduction:** **Low.** Meta-analytic signal (R5 0.81) but driven by mixed populations;
  imprecision.
- **CV death / stroke (individually):** **Low / inconclusive** — meta-analysis shows **no significant effect**;
  composite is carried by MI + all-cause. *This is the contradiction the draft must not paper over.*
- **Weight / BP / lipids (surrogates):** **High** — large, consistent, low-RoB (STEP, SELECT, R6).

## Contradiction gap (foreground this)
The headline "GLP-1 RA cut cardiovascular events by ~20%" rests on a **composite** MACE. When decomposed
(R5): MI ↓ and all-cause mortality ↓, but **CV death and stroke are not significantly reduced**. Reporting
the 20% as if all components moved would overstate the evidence. Honest framing: benefit is real but
**driven by MI and overall mortality, not by stroke or CV death**.

## Evidence gap (foreground this)
No RCT enrolls a **true primary-prevention** (no established CVD) non-diabetic population with MACE as the
endpoint. SELECT = secondary prevention; the primary-prevention inference is **extrapolation** (consistent
with R4 Stumpf calling it "scarce and controversial"). Surrogate-only trials (STEP, R6) show metabolic
benefit but were not powered for events.

## Assumption Register (→ Limitations)
1. Assuming SELECT's established-CVD result extrapolates to lower-risk primary-prevention obesity — **unproven**.
2. Assuming meta-analytic pooling (R5/R7) across heterogeneous GLP-1 RA regimens applies to semaglutide
   specifically — partially supported (semaglutide is the dominant contributor).
3. Assuming Western-trial results apply to **Vietnamese/Asian** patients — **no local data found**; BMI
   thresholds and body composition differ in Asian populations (flag explicitly).
4. Assuming high trial-discontinuation (16.6%, R1) does not erode real-world effectiveness — uncertain.

## Per-claim strength labels (for the writer)
- "Semaglutide reduces MACE in non-diabetic obesity with established CVD" → **[GRADE Moderate]**
- "Benefit extends to primary prevention" → **[GRADE Low — extrapolated]**
- "Reduces CV death/stroke specifically" → **[GRADE Low / not demonstrated]**
- "Produces large weight loss + BP/lipid improvement" → **[GRADE High]**
- Any Vietnam-specific efficacy claim → **[no evidence — do not state]**
