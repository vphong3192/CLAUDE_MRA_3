ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction is common in atrial fibrillation (AF), driven by an underlying left
atrial (LA) cardiomyopathy. We hypothesize that advancing age is a key risk factor for the development of LA cardiomyopathy
and heart failure with preserved ejection fraction in patients with AF.
OBJECTIVE This study aimed to determine the impact of age on invasive and noninvasive measures of LA structure and
function.
METHODS Consecutive patients with symptomatic AF with preserved left ventricular (LV) function undergoing catheter
ablation were enrolled. During ablation, invasive hemodynamic assessment was performed to quantify LA pressure and stiff-
ness. Noninvasive assessment included LA reservoir strain and indexed volumes, LV global longitudinal strain, LV end-
diastolic volume, and natriuretic peptides. Functional capacity was determined using cardiopulmonary exercise testing, and
disease-specific patient-reported symptoms were assessed. Results were adjusted for common risk factors.
RESULTS Of 125 patients, advancing age was associated with increased LA pressure (β 5 0.08, P 5 .05) and stiffness (β 5 0.10,
P 5 .002). Patients older than 65 years had greater LA stiffness (P < .001), but no difference in LA pressure (P 5 .11). Noninvasive
measures revealed reduced LA reservoir strain (β 5 20.37, P < .001), increased LA minimum (β 5 0.24, P < .001) and maximum
volumes (β 5 0.24, P 5 .007), reduced LV end-diastolic volume (β 5 20.63, P 5 .005), and increased natriuretic peptides
(β 5 12.8, P 5 .01) with age. Age was associated with reduced peak oxygen consumption (β 5 20.15, P 5 .02), but not AF
(β 5 20.01, P 5 .66) or heart failure symptoms (β 5 20.13, P 5 .49).
CONCLUSION In patients with symptomatic AF, aging is associated with LA hemodynamic, structural, and functional
impairments, suggestive of more advanced LA disease. These age-related changes were confirmed with exercise intolerance
but not patient-reported symptoms.

Introduction
Atrial fibrillation (AF) is an increasingly prevalent atrial arryth-
mia.1 Advancing age is the leading risk factor for the develop-
ment of AF, followed by elevated cardiometabolic risk factor
burden such as obesity and hypertension, which become
more prevalent with increasing age.2–4
Aging is associated with left atrial (LA) structural and
electrical remodeling that predisposes toward AF.5 With
advancing age, there is well-established evidence of reduced
atrial voltages, slowed conduction, and sinus node dysfunc-
tion, all of which may contribute to the development of the
atrial substrate underlying AF.5

Heart failure has become the most frequent complication
in patients with AF, with a lifetime risk twice that of stroke.6
Heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF) is the
most common form of incident heart failure.7 In patients
with symptomatic AF, almost three-quarters present with
invasive hemodynamic evidence of HFpEF.8 However, there
has been little evaluation of the association between
advancing age and hemodynamic evidence of LA functional
impairment, derived from both invasive and noninvasive
indices. We hypothesize that older patients with symptom-
atic AF have more advanced functional impairment of the
LA, which may predispose to a greater risk of heart failure.
This study aimed to (1) determine the effects of age on
invasive hemodynamic assessment of LA pressure and stiff-
ness, (2) determine the effects of age on noninvasive echo-
cardiographic LA reservoir strain and volumes, and (3)
determine the effects of age on functional limitations and
patient-reported AF and heart failure symptoms.

This prospective clinical study took place at the Centre for
Heart Rhythm Disorder at the University of Adelaide. All par-

Ethics Committee of the Central Adelaide Local Health

The study was registered with the Australian and New Zea-

Transthoracic echocardiography
For noninvasive assessment of LA and LV structure and func-
tion, all patients underwent a transthoracic echocardiogram.
Images were obtained from biplane apical views by an expe-
rienced sonographer, according to the American Society of
Echocardiography guidelines.12,13 Imaging was averaged
over 3 cardiac cycles in sinus rhythm and over 6 cycles in
AF. Structural measures included LA minimum (end-diastole)
and maximum (end-systole) volumes indexed to body surface
area, and LV end-diastolic volume. Functional measures
included LA reservoir strain and LV global longitudinal strain.
The LA-to-LV volume ratio was performed using the LA end-
diastolic volume and LV end-diastolic volume. Analysis of
strain was performed using a previously validated software.14
Cardiopulmonary exercise test
Cardiorespiratory fitness was measured using cardiopulmo-
nary exercise testing (CPET) performed on an upright cycle
ergometer (Lode Corival, Lode B.V.). Rate control medication
was withheld before CPET. Pulmonary gas exchange was
measured continuously using a metabolic cart (Vyntus CPX,
Vyaire Medical), whereas a 12-lead electrocardiogram was
monitored throughout. Blood pressure was recorded in 3-
minute intervals. Patients began cycling at a power of 20
W, increasing by 10 W/min. Peak exercise was defined as
the point the patient stopped owing to fatigue or symptoms.
Oxygen consumption (VO2) and carbon dioxide (CO2) pro-
duction (VCO2) were averaged over 20-second intervals,
with simultaneous calculation of ventilatory efficiency of

Blood sampling
A venous blood sample was collected at rest, in a fasted
state, in the week before catheter ablation. From this sample,
N-terminal pro–B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) was
quantified using a commercially available assay. The cardiac
rhythm at the time of collection was reported.
Statistical analysis
All statistical analyses were performed using R Studio version
4.3.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna,
Austria). Continuous variables are presented as mean 6 stan-
dard deviation or median (interquartile range) as appro-
priate. Between-group differences were compared using
independent t tests or Mann-Whitney U tests for continuous
data and χ2 or Fisher’s exact tests for categorical data. The
associations between advancing age and LA invasive hemo-
dynamics, noninvasive echocardiography, CPET, patient-
reported symptoms, and pathology parameters were
analyzed using multivariable linear regression models, ad-
justing for key covariates including age, sex, body mass index
(BMI), hypertension, type 2 diabetes, and persistent AF. Age
was analyzed both as a continuous variable and dichoto-
mized at 65 years for subgroup comparisons. P values were
2 sided, with statistical significance set at P 5 .05.

Age and invasive hemodynamics in AF
In this current study using a novel, invasive assessment of LA
pressure and stiffness during fluid challenge, we show that
advancing age is associated with elevated LA pressures and
stiffness. Similarly, LA stiffness computed from resting LA
pressure and previous LA imaging has previously been asso-
ciated with older age and future recurrence of AF after an
ablation.18 We propose that impaired LA hemodynamics dur-
ing fluid challenge may represent a more advanced atrial dis-
ease potentially associated with downstream complications
such as stroke and heart failure. Indeed, impaired LA hemo-
dynamics have been implicated in the development of
HFpEF among patients with AF.8

Age and functional capacity in AF
The age-related changes
in LA hemodynamics were
observed alongside differences in functional capacity. CPET
revealed significantly decreased VO2peak, suggesting that
older patients have more profound exercise intolerance.
Although the reduction of aerobic capacity with increasing
age is well established, reduced exercise tolerance in aging
patients with AF has previously been associated with
increased LV filling pressures, each with clinical significance
for subclinical HFpEF.19 The negative implications of
reduced aerobic capacity extend to rhythm status, with exer-
cise intolerance associated with reduced maintenance of
sinus rhythm,20,21 even among obese individuals.22 The rela-
tionship between exercise intolerance and LA dysfunction
has previously been established. Healthy LA contractile func-
tion contributes 15%–30% of LV stroke volume; however, in
AF with loss of LA contractile function, this augmented effect
is lost.23 This extends to patients with AF in sinus rhythm, with
the underlying atrial disease impairing LA function and
contributing to the reduction in exercise capacity.24 Similar
findings have been reported among patients with HFpEF,
with LA conduit function significantly impaired and associ-

Age and AF and heart failure symptoms
Clinical implications
Despite an advanced atrial disease, we did not see an asso-
ciation between advancing age and AF- or heart failure–
specific symptoms. This observation suggests that symptom
evaluation may not reflect the extent of LA cardiomyopathy
in older patients. Our findings come in contrast to previous
findings using noninvasive assessment, in which the degree
of diastolic dysfunction was associated with advancing age
and increasing AF symptom severity.26 Similarly, atrial
dysfunction has been reported to contribute to heart failure
symptoms among older patients with HFpEF.27 One poten-
tial explanation for our findings is that many older patients
with AF present with numerous comorbidities and therefore
varied symptoms. Patients with complex interrelated symp-
toms may find it difficult to accurately identify the specific co-
morbidity that is causing their symptoms and may not
attribute them to AF or heart failure. In support of our find-
ings, a recent meta-analysis evaluating major clinical out-
comes in symptomatic and asymptomatic AF found no
difference in cardiovascular mortality, thromboembolism,
stroke, hospitalization, and myocardial infarction in those
with or without AF symptoms.28 This suggests that AF- or
heart failure–specific symptoms may not be related to the
underlying LA disease, and the use of specific imaging and
functional testing may be beneficial in this cohort.
Our study highlights the significant role of advancing age in
the development of an atrial cardiomyopathy through
impaired LA hemodynamics and both structural and func-
tional abnormalities among patients with AF. The distinct
LA abnormalities revealed from the invasive and noninvasive
assessments are not necessarily aligned with patient clinical
status.
Performing this simple hemodynamic assessment during
the ablation procedure provides additional insight and allows
for the quantification of a potential underlying atrial cardio-
myopathy. Age-related LA remodeling may contribute to
the increased risk of AF-related complications such as HFpEF,
stroke, and dementia, with careful LA assessment in older pa-
tients helping to identify those at greater risk. Although aging
is nonmodifiable, addressing modifiable risk factors such as
cardiorespiratory fitness may offset the effects on the LA
and reduce the risk of AF-related complications.29
Study limitations
This study has several limitations that should be acknowl-
edged. First, the cross-sectional design allows for clinical as-
sociation; however, it is unable to infer causality. Second, our
study cohort was limited to patients with AF undergoing
ablation given the need for LA access, subsequently

Therefore, our findings may not be generalizable to all pa-
tients with AF. Third, although we adjusted for sex, hyperten-
sion, diabetes, BMI, and AF type, confounders may still
influence the associations present. Longitudinal studies are
warranted to confirm these findings and better understand
the temporal relationship between advancing age and LA
cardiomyopathy.
Conclusion
In patients with symptomatic AF, advanced age was associ-
ated with invasively assessed increases in LA pressure and
stiffness, with those older than 65 years demonstrating the
greatest impairments. These findings were confirmed with
noninvasive echocardiographic measures of structure and
function and functional testing, but not patient-reported AF
or heart failure symptoms.
