ABSTRACT
Background: Age is a relevant risk factor for the development of atrial
ﬁbrillation (AF) and is associated with increased recurrence rates in the
setting of rhythm control. Catheter ablation is increasingly advocated
in elderly despite conﬂicting data regarding its efﬁcacy and safety in
this patient cohort. Therefore, we aimed to analyse currently available
evidence regarding catheter ablation for AF in patients (cid:1) 75 years old
compared with younger patients.
Methods: We performed a systematic literature search and meta-
analysis on efﬁcacy and safety of catheter ablation in patients (cid:1) 75
years old with AF. Primary efﬁcacy and safety end points were ﬁrst
recurrence of atrial arrhythmia after ﬁrst-time ablation and occurrence
of death, stroke, or any procedure-related complication. Secondary
outcomes included procedure and ﬂuoroscopy time.
Results: We identiﬁed 301 potentially relevant studies, of which 39
underwent detailed analysis. A total of 19 studies (MINORS score (cid:1)
13) reporting on 108,419 patients (101,844 < 75 years, 6,575 (cid:1) 75
years of age) undergoing ﬁrst-time catheter ablation for AF were
included. Risk of arrhythmia recurrence after catheter ablation (39% vs
32%, relative risk [RR] 1.24, 95% conﬁdence interval [CI] 1.09-1.41;
P ¼ 0.001) and occurrence of safety end points (10.8% vs 8.5%; RR
1.64, 95% CI 1.53-1.76; P < 0.00001) were signiﬁcantly higher in
patients (cid:1) 75 years of age than in younger patients. There was no
difference concerning procedure (P ¼ 0.33) or ﬂuoroscopy time (P ¼
0.91) between younger and elderly patients.
Conclusions: In patients (cid:1) 75 years of age, catheter ablation for AF
has higher risk of arrhythmia recurrence and is associated with an
increased risk of procedure-related complications and safety end point
occurrence compared with younger patients.

Methods
Study selection
A comprehensive search for catheter ablation in elderly
patients was performed in scientiﬁc databases (PubMed,
Cochrane, Medline) and Google Scholar. To identify and
retrieve all potentially relevant studies regarding this topic, the
search was performed with the terms “catheter ablation,”
“pulmonary vein isolation,” “old,” “elderly,” “75 years,” “80
years,” “octogenarian,” “geriatric,” and “atrial ﬁbrillation.”
Reference lists and review articles were reviewed to identify
additional relevant articles.
Reports of clinical studies comparing outcomes between
patients (cid:1) 75 years old and younger patients, published as
original articles in English in peer-reviewed journals were
considered for the meta-analysis if the following inclusion
criteria were met: 1) inclusion of patients (cid:1) 75 years under-
going ﬁrst-time catheter ablation for AF, 2) availability of data
regarding atrial arrhythmia recurrence or procedural compli-
cations, 3) minimum follow-up duration (cid:1) 6 months for
studies on efﬁcacy, and 4) effect sizes given as risk ratio (RR)
or calculation of risk ratio possible.
All eligible and potentially relevant studies were indepen-
dently evaluated by 2 experienced reviewers
(J.R.E and
B.M.K.) with divergences resolved by discussion among all
authors of this meta-analysis.
Methodologic quality of selected studies was independently
assessed by both reviewers using the MINORS (Methodo-
logical Index for Nonrandomised Studies) scoring system,
with a maximum value of 24 points to achieve for comparative
studies.46 Scoring criteria include the following aspects (0 to 2
points per criterion): a clearly stated study aim, inclusion of
consecutive patients, prospective data collection, end points
appropriate to the aim of the study, unbiased assessment of
study end points, appropriateness of follow-up time after
diagnosis, loss to follow-up < 5%, prospective calculation of
study size, adequate control group, contemporary groups (no
historical comparison), baseline equivalence of groups, and
appropriate statistical analysis.46 The study quality was cat-
egorised as follows: > 16 points, high-quality; 13-16 points,
moderate quality; (cid:3) 12 points,
low-quality. The average

Statistical analyses
Statistical analyses were performed with the Review Man-
ager Version 5.4 (RevMan; The Cochrane Collaboration,
Copenhagen, Denmark)
and PRISM software

Distribution of enrolled patients and patient characteristics
are presented as mean and standard deviation where appli-
cable. Dichotomous baseline characteristics were pooled and
divided by the total number of patient data available for the
characteristics to determine the proportion of the character-
istic in the total population studied. In addition, the variables
were compared using chi-square test.
RRs for categoric variables and weighted mean differences
(WMDs) for continuous variables, with inherent 95% con-
ﬁdence interval (CIs) served for the comparison of catheter
ablation in younger vs elderly patients. The Mantel-Haenszel
random effects model (DerSimonian and Laird47) was used to
calculate pooled RRs for categoric variables, and the inverse
variance random effects model was used to calculate pooled
mean differences for continuous variables. The random effects
model was checked against ﬁxed effects to avoid inﬂuence of
small studies. The Breslow-Day chi2 test and the I2 statistic
were used to test heterogeneity across the studies. I2 values
< 25% indicated low, 25%-50% moderate, and > 50% high
heterogeneity.48 The restricted maximum likelihood method
(tau-square) tested between-study heterogeneity.
The risk estimates of primary efﬁcacy and safety outcomes
were calculated for the overall population according to age
(< 75 years and (cid:1) 75 years) and with an additional sub-
analysis on AF type. If 3 age groups were speciﬁed in reports,
the lower 2 were combined if possible and compared with the
older one.
An inﬂuence analysis was performed to evaluate if any of
the included studies altered the summary of RRs for primary
outcomes, and a random effects sensitivity analysis evaluated
the extent to which deﬁnition of elderly or AF type studied
might have inﬂuenced the risk estimates for end points in case
of signiﬁcant heterogeneity.

Discussion
Principal ﬁndings
This updated meta-analysis of 19 studies examined efﬁcacy
and safety of catheter ablation for the treatment of AF between
patients (cid:1) 75 years of age and younger patients. The main re-
sults are that compared with their younger counterparts, pa-
tients (cid:1) 75 years old have w 25% higher arrhythmia recurrence
risk and w 65% higher complication rate after ﬁrst-time cath-
eter ablation of AF. Data regarding the effects among speciﬁc AF
entities are limited but indicate highest arrhythmia recurrence
risk in elderly patients with persistent AF.
Although the efﬁcacy and superiority of catheter ablation
for maintaining sinus rhythm50 and the beneﬁts of early
rhythm control for reducing cardiovascular end points7 have

Limitations
This meta-analysis is subject to all potential limitations of
systematic reviews and meta-analyses, including the decision on
sensitivity analyses, for which there are no ﬁxed guidelines.
Because we did not have access to most of the original patient-
level data, we had to rely on published data or information
obtained from the study authors during the analysis process.
Patients (cid:1) 75 years of age had a signiﬁcantly higher cardio-
vascular comorbidity burden. Because of limited data availability,
adjustments for these comorbidities or AF type was not possible,
and although some studies in this meta-analysis suggest an in-
dependent relationship between arrhythmia recurrence after AF
ablation and age,31,36 some uncertainties remain regarding the
particular inﬂuence of cardiovascular comorbidities.
Despite the fact that deﬁnitions of efﬁcacy and safety end
points were largely consistent, there were differences in the
monitoring strategies and data quality of the studies. The
majority of studies lacked speciﬁc information on time since AF
diagnosis, time in AF before ablation, and AF burden, which
would have facilitated the interpretation and understanding of
the results of this meta-analysis. In addition, data are currently
available only on the use of thermal ablation strategies in
older patients, and results on pulsed-ﬁeld ablation are not yet
available for this patient population.
Finally, it should be noted that the studies included in this
meta-analysis were mostly conducted by academic centres
with careful follow-up strategies and that patients (cid:1) 75 years
of age undergoing catheter ablation may represent a highly
selective collective of patients with AF. Therefore, this meta-
analysis may not reﬂect the results of “real-world” ablation
practice. Furthermore, no parameters regarding quality of life
could be extracted from the underlying studies.
Nonetheless, this meta-analysis represents the best possible
approximation of ablation outcomes in patients (cid:1) 75 years of
age at the present time.
