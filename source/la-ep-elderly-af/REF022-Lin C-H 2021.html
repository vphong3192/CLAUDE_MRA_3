Atrial fibrillation (AF) is the most common sustained cardiac arrhythmia, and its prevalence
increases with age.1 Catheter ablation is a crucial method for treating AF.2, 3 Kistler et al
demonstrated that aging is associated with atrial remodeling and is represented by regional
conduction slowing, structural changes including low-voltage areas, and increased atrial
20
refractoriness. Atrial remodeling may be responsible for the increase in the propensity to atrial
arrhythmias that accompanies an increase in age.4 Our previous study ever demonstrated that age

Methods
Study Patients
This is a retrospective observational study of patients with drug-refractory AF. The study
protocol was reviewed by the local ethics committee (number: 2020-10-012BC). From Sep 2003 to
10
Feb 2016, a total of 1585 consecutive patients over 20 years-old(1181 paroxysmal AF [PAF] and 404
non-paroxysmal AF [non-PAF]) who had received catheter ablation for AF guided by the 3D mapping
system were enrolled in this study. The patients were divided into 3 groups according to their ages
(young, 20-40 years-old, n = 175; middle-aged, 41-64 years-old, n = 1134; and old, ≥65 years-old, n =
276). The anteroposterior diameter of the LA at end-systole was measured through M-mode
15
echocardiography in the parasternal short axis view by 2 independent observers.6 All the patients
signed a consent form for the electrophysiology study and catheter ablation. Non-PAF was defined
as persistent AF or long-standing persistent AF.
Electrophysiological Study and Catheter Ablation
The details of the electrophysiological study have been described in the previous report.2, 7, 8 In
20
brief, each patient underwent electrophysiological examination in the fasting, non-sedated state

3

after providing informed consent. All antiarrhythmic drugs except amiodarone were discontinued for
at least 5 half-lives before the electrophysiological study and ablation procedure commenced.
Our ablation techniques for AF originating from the NPV have been demonstrated in the
literature.9 In brief, in patients with PAF, we attempted to record spontaneous onset of ectopic
5
activity initiating AF before or after the infusion of isoproterenol (1–4 μg/min). If atrial ectopy did
not occur, intermittent burst pacing from right atrium(RA) and coronary sinus(CS) with brief pauses
in between each burst, or high-dose intravenous adenosine bolus would be used. If AF still could not
appear, burst pacing would be performed until sustained AF was induced. Following that, external
cardioversion was deployed to restore AF to sinus rhythm. After this point, any spontaneous
10
re-initiation of AF would be observed. To ensure the reproducibility of AF induction by these
methods, AF would be induced at least twice to confirm the trigger. AF triggers could be found in all
patients in this study cohort.
In patients with non- PAF, the stepwise procedure of catheter ablation was performed, and the
details have been described in our previous work.10 In brief, isolation of the PVs was performed as
15
the first step. Continuous circumferential lesions were created encircling the right and left PV ostia
guided by the 3D mapping system using an irrigated- tip ablation catheter to obtain the entrance
and exit block of the PVs. Successful circumferential PV isolation was demonstrated by the absence
of any electrical activity inside the PV or dissociated PV activity during AF. After PV isolation,
additional linear ablation and/or continuous complex- fractionated atrial electrogram site ablation
20
were performed. If the AF did not terminate after these procedures, sinus rhythm(SR) was restored
by electrical cardioversion, and spontaneous ectopic beats initiating AF were identified, as in
patients with PAF.

For the mapping of non-PV ectopic beats, we evaluated the activation sequence of the high RA,
His bundle area, and CS. The time interval (<0 millisecond) between the high RA and His bundle area
during sinus rhythm and ectopy can differentiate the site of ectopy as the superior vena cava (SVC),
upper crista terminalis, or PV. If the earliest activation site was in the vicinity of the interatrial
5
septum, simultaneous mapping of the right and left septum was performed. For the non-PV triggers
from the LA, the activation time interval between the proximal and distal pairs of the CS decapolar
catheter during ectopy was evaluated to determine the location of the ectopic foci near the right (>0
millisecond) or left (<0 millisecond) PV ostium. If the earliest activation site was in the vicinity of the
left PV ostium or posterolateral portion of the mitral annulus, potentials from the ligament of
10
Marshall (LOM) were clarified by differential pacing or epicardial mapping. Cavotricuspid isthmus
ablation was performed using an 8-mm tip ablation catheter. The SVC triggers were ablated by SVC
isolation. Other non-PV triggers were also ablated.
Atrial Substrate Analysis with 3D Mapping System
As described in our previous work,11 bi-atrial electroanatomic maps and sequential contact
15
voltage maps were constructed in patients during SR. The LA and RA bipolar peak-to-peak voltages
were obtained from more than 150 sites approximately equally distributed within the atria before
catheter ablation. The electrodes of the CS catheter that were used to provide the timing reference
signal were maintained in a stable position throughout the mapping procedure. The absolute peak
was selected as the detection setting to determine the point of activation in the waveform. The
20
ablation catheter and spiral catheter signals were used to record local activation times (relative to
the reference signal) during SR. The total activation time (TAT) was defined as the time interval from
This article is protected by copyright. All rights reserved.
5



9019.199999999999Page 6
the earliest to the latest activation point in the atrium. After completion of the sequential map,
bipolar mapping points were collected and analyzed using an offline software.7
Follow-Up of AF symptomatic Recurrence Events
After discharge, the patients were followed up (2 weeks after catheter ablation, then every 1-3
5
months) at our cardiology clinic or by referring physicians, and antiarrhythmic drugs were prescribed
to prevent the early recurrence of PAF. When the patients experienced symptoms suggesting a
tachycardia event , a 24-h Holter monitoring or cardiac event recording for 1 week was performed
for to determine. AF recurrence was defined as an episode, confirmed by an electrocardiogram,
lasting for >30 s after ablation. Early, late, and very late recurrence were defined as <3 month,
10
between 3 month and 1 year, and 1 year after the index ablation procedure, respectively.12, 13
Statistical Analysis
A Student’s test (if the obtained samples were normally distributed) or Kruskal-Wallis test (if the
obtained samples were not normally distributed) were used for the continuous data. The values for
each patient are reported as the mean ± SD. Parametric data are reported as the mean ± standard
15
deviation and analyzed using one-way ANOVA. The chi-square test with the Yates correction was
used to analyze categorical data. A value of P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All
statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software, version 22.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA).

Main Findings
iscussion
D
5
In the patients with AF, age affects trigger foci and the outcomes of catheter ablation. Our
findings were as follows: (1) The incidence of only non-PV foci was higher in the young group than in
the other groups; (2) Non-PV foci in the SVC were more frequently observed in the young group than
10
in the other groups; (3) The mean peak-to-peak voltage of the LA was higher in the young and
middle-aged groups than in the old group; and (4) Among the PAF patients with both non-PV foci
and PV foci, the very late symptomatic recurrence rate after multiple procedures was lower in the
young group than in the other groups.
Effect of Age on the Trigger foci of AF
15
Most ectopic beats that initiate PAF originated from the PV.2, 14 Several studies have addressed
the importance of non-PV foci in PAF, which tend to be located at the sites such as the SVC, LA free
wall, crista terminalis, CS ostium, ligament of Marshall, LA appendage, and interatrial septum.7, 15
Female sex , younger age, and LA enlargement are predictors of the presence of non-PV ectopic
beats.16, 17 Among the patients with non-PV-initiating AF, most triggers were located in SVC.17 In a
20
large series of 836 patients who had undergone AF ablation,18 patients with arrhythmogenic SVC
were typically young, had a low incidence of obesity, hypertension and structural heart disease. In
9
This article is protected by copyright. All rights reserved.



16154.559999999998Page 10
another study of 79 patients, compared with patients with AF with PV triggers, these with AF
initiated by non-PV trigger in RA were younger.19 The application of RF energy in non-PV triggers is
effective and safe for treating AF.7 To our knowledge, no large-scale studies investigating the
incidence of trigger foci in different age groups are currently available. For the first time, our study
5
results showed that the incidence of only non-PV foci (more commonly located in the SVC) was
higher in the young patients than in the middle-aged and old patients. Furthermore, the third fat
pad, the gate keeper ganglion of the cardiac parasympathetic nerve, is located at the junction of the
SVC, RA septum, and ascending aorta.20 The autonomic nervous system may play a crucial role in
young patients with AF.21This evidence may partially explain our findings.
10
There was another study with 573 consecutive female patients with AF, and it revealed
significantly higher number of non-PV triggers were detected in women ≥75 years-old, compared
with women < 75 years-old. 22This result was different from ours, and this may be due to male-
predominant population in our study(Table 1). The sex-difference may have substantial influence on
the mechanism and the ablation result of AF.
15

association between AF and several single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) on chromosome 4q25.23
The locus is adjacent to the paired-like homeodomain transcription factor 2 (PITX2) which is
important for PV development.24
One SNPs, rs2200733 close to PITX, was associated with early-onset

negatively associated.26 The SNPs over chromosome 4q25 could possibly be the genetic factors of

Effect of Age on the Atrial Substrate
Experimental studies in animal models have shown that aging is associated with a significant
5
increase in the dispersion of action potential parameters in the atrium.27 Aging is related with atrial,
electrical, and structural remodeling, which may explain the increased propensity to atrial
arrhythmias that accompanies an increase in age.4 Another study showed that the benefits of PV
isolation extend equally to all age groups without recurrence after catheter ablation.28 In a previous
study with 350 patients with AF, the LA bipolar voltage was significantly lower in older patients than
10
in younger patients; however, this difference did not occur in RA.5 Our study demonstrated that the
LA voltage was higher in the young group than in the old group and that the LA voltage decreased
with an increase in age; these findings were consistent with those of other studies.5, 27 The possible
explanation is that the substrate may deteriorate due to the remodeling of AF and underlying
disease with an increase in age. Besides, among the patients with non-PAF, the young group had a
15
lower RA mean voltage. The possible explanation could be more non-PV foci located in the right
atrial area such as SVC in the young age group, which may be associated with more abnormal
substrate property in RA.
Effect of Age on the Ablation Outcomes
20
One study showed that the incidence of AF recurrence was higher in older patients than in
younger patients.5 Our previous study demonstrated that LA scarring was an independent factor for

ablation. In our present study, very late symptomatic recurrence was lower in the young than in the

This is a retrospective, and observational study, as well as a homogeneous population with
male-predominance. Its applicability to other populations, especially the female patients, requires
further confirmation. An extensive left and right atrium ablation before looking for non PV foci may
have influenced the mapping results in non-paroxysmal AF patients. The period of enrollment in this
study is long. It is likely that patients enrolled in the late phase of the study had better outcome than
15
those enrolled in the early phase because advanced technologies were available during the late
phase of the study. The phase of enrollment may impact the ablation outcome. Finally, this study is
in lack of the genetic survey, which is important for the mechanism study.
